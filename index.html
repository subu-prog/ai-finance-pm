Financial controlling

The biggest wins are in variance commentary, the close and forecasting, because these repeat every month and run on structured data that already exists.

Budgeting and planning Medium effort

Builds a first-draft budget from history, drivers and growth assumptions, and runs what-if scenarios in plain language.

Value: faster cycles, more scenarios tested
Tools: Anaplan, Pigment, Workday Adaptive, Claude or ChatGPT with Excel

Rolling forecasting Medium effort

Machine-learning forecasts of revenue, cost and cash, blended with driver-based models, with an explanation of what moved.

Value: better accuracy, earlier warning on misses
Tools: SAP Analytics Cloud, Oracle EPM, Python (Prophet, scikit-learn), Microsoft Fabric

Variance analysis Low effort

An agent compares actuals with budget and forecast, finds the top drivers, and drafts the management commentary.

Value: hours of commentary cut to minutes, consistent explanations
Tools: LLM agent on ERP or BI extracts, Power BI Copilot, Datarails

Month-end close Medium effort

Auto-matches reconciliations, suggests accruals, tracks the close checklist and chases owners.

Value: shorter close, fewer late adjustments
Tools: BlackLine, FloQast, Trintech, SAP Joule, custom agents

Management reporting Low effort

Generates the monthly pack, board slides and narrative from one data source, and answers ad-hoc questions in natural language.

Value: self-service for managers, fewer report requests
Tools: Power BI Copilot, Tableau Pulse, Claude or ChatGPT on the data warehouse

Controls and audit Medium effort

Scans every journal entry and payment for anomalies, duplicates and policy breaks, and drafts control evidence.

Value: earlier fraud and error detection, cheaper audits
Tools: MindBridge, AppZen, Oversight, Python anomaly models

Spend and cost analytics Low effort

Classifies spend, spots savings and contract leakage, and explains cost-centre trends.

Value: direct cost savings
Tools: Coupa, SAP Ariba AI, custom LLM classification

Three agents worth building first

  1. Variance commentary agent. Reads the monthly actual-vs-budget extract, ranks the drivers, and drafts commentary for each cost centre. The controller reviews and edits instead of writing from scratch.
  2. Close copilot. Tracks the close calendar, flags open reconciliations, and sends reminders to owners in Teams or Slack.
  3. Finance Q&A assistant. Lets managers ask "why is marketing over budget in Q3?" and get an answer grounded in the actual numbers, with links to the source.
Rollout path: pilot on one business unit with exported data and human review of every output, then connect to the ERP or data warehouse with read-only access, then embed in the monthly calendar with a sign-off step and audit log.

Project management

AI pays off most on status reporting and risk detection. Project managers spend a large share of their week gathering updates and writing reports that an agent can draft from the tools the team already uses.

Planning and scheduling Low effort

Turns a scope document into a work breakdown, task list and draft schedule, and estimates durations from past projects.

Value: faster kickoff, more realistic plans
Tools: MS Project with Copilot, Jira AI, Asana AI, Claude or ChatGPT

Status reporting Low effort

An agent reads Jira, Asana, MS Project, Teams and email, then drafts the weekly status report and RAG rating.

Value: an estimated 2–4 hours a week saved per PM, consistent reporting
Tools: custom LLM agent, Atlassian Intelligence, Notion AI, Monday AI

Risk and issue management Medium effort

Flags slipping tasks, scope creep and negative sentiment in team chatter, and keeps the risk register up to date.

Value: problems surface weeks earlier
Tools: custom agents, Jira AI, Planview, Clarity PPM

Resource planning Medium effort

Matches people to tasks by skill and availability, and forecasts over- and under-allocation.

Value: higher utilisation, fewer burnouts
Tools: Planview, Float, Runn, Python optimisation

Meetings and communication Low effort

Transcribes meetings, extracts decisions and action items, and pushes them into the project tool.

Value: no lost actions, less admin
Tools: Teams Copilot, Zoom AI, Otter, Fireflies

Portfolio prioritisation Medium effort

Scores projects on value, cost, risk and strategic fit, and runs scenarios for the portfolio board.

Value: better investment decisions
Tools: Planview, ServiceNow SPM, custom scoring models

Knowledge and lessons learned Low effort

A searchable assistant over past project files, so new PMs can ask "how did we handle vendor delays last time?"

Value: fewer repeated mistakes, faster onboarding
Tools: Claude Projects, ChatGPT Enterprise, Glean, SharePoint Copilot

Three agents worth building first

  1. Weekly status agent. Pulls updates from Jira or MS Project and Teams, drafts the status report, and suggests the RAG rating with reasons.
  2. Risk radar. Watches schedule slippage, budget burn and open issues, and alerts the PM before a project turns red.
  3. Meeting-to-actions agent. Converts meeting transcripts into tasks with owners and dates in the project tool.
Rollout path: start with one project team and read-only connections, have the PM review every draft for 4 to 6 weeks, then extend across the portfolio with a standard report template.

Where finance and projects meet: project controlling

The strongest opportunity sits between the two functions, because project costs, schedules and forecasts usually live in separate systems that nobody reconciles in real time.

Earned value on autopilot

Combines timesheets, purchase orders and ERP actuals with progress from the project tool, and calculates CPI, SPI and estimate-at-completion every week instead of every quarter.

Project cost forecasting

Predicts final cost and margin per project from burn rate and similar past projects, and flags overruns early.

Capex and WIP tracking

Classifies project spend as capex or opex, and tracks work in progress and revenue recognition against milestones.

One narrative, two audiences

The same agent drafts the steering-committee status and the controller's financial commentary, so the numbers always match.

90-day rollout

First agents in daily use within 90 days, with two go/no-go gates so nothing scales before it proves its value.

1. Discover

Weeks 1 to 3
  • Pick 2 to 3 use cases
  • Map data in ERP and PM tools
  • Baseline hours and close days
Gate 1
Data access and baseline agreed

2. Pilot

Weeks 4 to 8
  • Build variance and status agents on exported data
  • Review every output by hand
Gate 2
Time saved, accuracy signed off

3. Scale

Weeks 9 to 13
  • Connect ERP and PM tools read-only
  • Embed in the monthly calendar
  • Train controllers and PMs
Throughout: human review, read-only access first, audit log, data privacy

Success measures

Hours saved per month, close duration in days, forecast error, report turnaround time, and user adoption. No AI output posts to the ledger or goes to leadership without human sign-off, and sensitive data stays inside approved enterprise tools.
